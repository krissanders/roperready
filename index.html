Exam-day facts (from SPRAT's own scheme)

  • Written: 50 questions · 80% pass · 1 hour · open-book · within 10 days before the practical · two attempts allowed.
  • Practical grading: one Fail = out. Three Discrepancies = out. The evaluator explains each call before your next exercise.
  • L3 practical adds: system analysis, deviation/re-anchor build + rescue, knot-pass raise/lower, tensioned rope system, rescue from 2 of 5 maneuvers, and a written equipment-inspection report on deliberately damaged kit.
  • Certification runs 3 years; recert inside the final 6 months keeps your original anniversary date.

Grading day

1 Fail = evaluation over. 3 Discrepancies = evaluation over. Fail = critical safety issue (risk of serious injury). Discrepancy = non-critical (risk of minor injury). Written test within 10 days before practical, 2 attempts, 80%. Access work plan = work method + risk assessment + rescue plan. A rescue plan is mandatory, always.

Suspension intolerance — the current answer

Fully conscious casualty: may be laid down. Semi-conscious or unconscious: recovery position. Standard airway-breathing-circulation first aid applies. The old "keep them sitting upright for 30 minutes" advice was reviewed and withdrawn (UK HSE research, 2008) — it had no supporting evidence and could be harmful. Every motionless-suspension casualty goes to hospital, with suspension intolerance flagged to medics. Waiting for rescue: keep the legs moving or knees elevated.
